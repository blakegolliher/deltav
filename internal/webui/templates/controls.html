Connections are requests in flight: S3 over HTTP/1.1 holds one request per connection. Idle keep-alive connections are not counted.

Alert only reports CNodes over the limit on these pages, in the API and in /metrics. Nothing is enforced.

Enforce also gives every Envoy a cap. At its cap an Envoy makes requests to that VIP wait; it does not fail them or send them elsewhere. How Envoy applies a cap has three consequences:

  1. The cap is each Envoy's own. An Envoy cannot see what other hosts hold, so the limit is divided: limit ÷ connected Envoys ÷ the most VIPs of the target on one CNode, rounded down but never below 1. A CNode with fewer VIPs gets less than the limit. Once Envoys × VIPs is more than the limit, the fleet can exceed it; that is still reported.
  2. A new cap makes every Envoy reconnect. The cap is part of the target's cluster config in Envoy, and changing it makes Envoy close and reopen its connections for that target as requests finish. So as Envoys come and go, a recomputed cap is sent only when it differs from the one in force by more than {{percent .Settings.LimitMinChange}}, and no sooner than {{seconds .Settings.LimitMinIntervalSeconds}} after the last. A limit you set here is sent at once.
  3. Each worker thread of an Envoy can hold one connection more than the cap. Envoy lets a worker with no connection to a VIP open one, whatever the cap says. The client unit tells the daemon how many workers its Envoy runs (DELTAV_ENVOY_WORKERS, which also sets Envoy's --concurrency), and the cap is lowered by that number less one, so the limit still holds. An Envoy started another way does not say, and can exceed its cap by its worker count less one.

Drain a CNode

A drained CNode's VIPs are withheld from every Envoy at once; requests in flight finish. Nothing is changed on the VAST cluster. A drain lasts until you undo it, daemon restarts included.
